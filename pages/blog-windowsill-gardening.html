Windowsill Herb Gardening & Scallion Regrowing for Kids

By Chef Liam Chen • August 12, 2026 • 2 Comments • Academy Tested
Windowsill Garden Station: Potted Genovese basil and water-sprouting scallions

You don't need a three-acre backyard farm to teach children where food comes from. A single sunny windowsill, three recycled glass jars, and vegetable scraps from last night's dinner table are all you need to ignite lifelong botanical curiosity.

Project 1: The 48-Hour Green Onion Fountain

This is the single most rewarding beginner experiment for young children because visible results occur overnight.

  1. When prepping dinner, save the white root ends of scallions (about 1.5 inches long).
  2. Stand them upright in a clear shot glass or small glass jar.
  3. Add enough room-temperature water to submerge the roots while leaving the top tip dry.
  4. The Result: Place on a sunny windowsill. Within 48 hours, bright emerald green stalks shoot upward over 1 inch per day! Have your child measure daily progress with a ruler.

Project 2: Cloning Fresh Basil by Stem Cuttings

Snip a 4-inch stem just beneath a leaf node. Strip off the bottom leaves, leaving only the top four. Submerge the stem in clean water on a windowsill. Within 7 to 10 days, fine white roots sprout from the node!

Transfer rooted stems into small terracotta pots filled with organic potting mix. In three weeks, your young chef will harvest enough fresh leaves for homemade pesto pizza night.

The 1/3 Harvesting Rule: Teach children to never harvest more than one-third of a plant's leaves at once. Leaving two-thirds intact allows the plant to continue photosynthesizing and regenerating new branches all season long!
Academy Recipe / Activity

Kid-Made Mortar & Pestle Windowsill Garden Pesto

Ingredients Checklist:
  • 2 packed cups fresh Genovese basil leaves (harvested from windowsill!)
  • 1/3 cup toasted pine nuts, sunflower seeds, or pepitas (nut-free)
  • 2 cloves fresh garlic, peeled
  • 1/2 cup grated Parmigiano-Reggiano cheese
  • 1/2 cup cold-pressed extra virgin olive oil
  • 1 pinch coarse sea salt
Step-by-Step Instructions:
  1. The Gentle Snip: Harvest top leaf clusters with safety scissors using the 1/3 rule.
  2. The Mortar Mash: Pound garlic and salt into a paste in mortar and pestle. Add basil leaves and bruise into deep emerald ribbons.
  3. The Olive Oil Drizzle: Slowly stir in cheese, seeds, and olive oil until fragrant and glossy. Spoon over warm pasta or pizza!
